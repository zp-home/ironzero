fix(bridge): make the pool-sorting anchor stable for the whole episode

resourceSlots() sorts the map's ore pools by distance to ownBaseAnchor(), and the
policy's slot_id is an index into that sorted list. If the anchor moves, every
slot_id silently names a different pool.

ownBaseAnchor() returned the first own unit for which isFactoryBuilding() was
true, with a comment claiming the command centre is unique. It is not:
isFactoryBuilding() is true for a landFactory too, and the loop walks
Unit.unitList in the engine's own order. The moment our first combat factory
finished, the anchor could jump from the command centre to that factory and
re-sort the pool list under the policy.

Measured with --debug-build on Lake at difficulty 1: the expansion stage asked for
"the nearest free pool" and the bridge built at (1170,2050) sixteen times, then
(1250,1190), then (1020,2315) -- the same index resolving to different places.
The builder's distance to its target then oscillated in the pattern
894 -> 1109 (walking away), switch, 977 -> 693, switch, 1184 -> 993, switch.

Fix: identify the command centre positively by its engine internal name and
prefer it outright; fall back to a factory building only when we own no command
centre; the last resort averages buildings only, not every living unit, because a
marching army drifted the anchor for the same reason the extra factory did.

Verified: the build coordinates no longer jump between places for a fixed slot
index, and 113 core tests pass.

Still not achieved: the economy chain does not close. probe_economy_chain.py over
1900 decisions (19000 ticks) reports best income 26.0, best occupied 1 of 9 pools,
peak army 6. Detail: pool geometry on this map puts the nearest *free* pool 976 px
from the builder, so a leg is ~1220 ticks of walking, and the stage still gives up
on most legs. Difficulty-1 win rate remains 0%.
